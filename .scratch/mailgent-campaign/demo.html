The problem

AI agents are great at email. Giving them your email is the scary part.

Today, connecting an assistant to your mail is usually all-or-nothing: one sign-in and it can read every message, every attachment, from every year — and you never see what it looked at.

📬

Everything, or nothing

You wanted help with this week's bills. The agent got your bank statements, medical letters and family email too.

🔑

Secrets ride along

Passwords, API keys and login links sitting in old email go wherever the agent goes.

🙈

No receipt

There's no simple way to see what the agent actually read, or to stop it with one click.

Made for real inboxes

One app, very different slices.

You decide the slice per agent. Here's what that looks like in practice.

Freelancer

“Which invoices are still unpaid this month?”

Allow the Invoices mailbox, Read mail. Personal mail stays off.

Developer

“Summarise today's CI alerts.”

Allow mail from your CI domain with leak guard on, so deploy keys come through as [REDACTED].

Busy parent

“What do I need to bring to school this week?”

Allow mail from the school's domain only, for the last 30 days.

Traveller

“When is my flight and where's the boarding pass?”

Full access to bookings, so the agent can open the PDF. Bank mail is blocked.

Straight talk

What MailGent is — and isn't.

It is

  • A companion beside Apple Mail, in your menu bar
  • A permission layer for AI agents you already use
  • On-device: index, rules and logs stay on your Mac
  • Open source (Apache-2.0)

It isn't

  • A new mail app — keep using Apple Mail
  • An AI assistant — it has no AI of its own
  • Able to send, move or delete mail (not in this alpha)
  • A finished product — it's an early alpha, provided as is
Get started

Five minutes to your first safe question.

  1. Download the alphaGrab MailGent-0.8.2.dmg from GitHub Releases and drag MailGent to Applications.
  2. Open it onceThe alpha isn't notarized yet, so macOS may warn you. Use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway, or right-click → Open.
  3. Let it read Apple MailGive Full Disk Access, or use “Choose Mail Folder…”. MailGent builds its search index on your Mac.
  4. Pair your agentCursor is paired for you on first launch. For Grok Bot, click Pair and paste the snippet into its settings.
  5. Allow a mailbox and askOpen Grant Desk, tick one mailbox, pick “Read mail”, then ask your agent. Check the Access Log afterwards.

Needs macOS 14 or later and Apple Mail with your mail already downloaded.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Does MailGent send my email anywhere?

No. MailGent has no server. It reads Apple Mail's files on your Mac to build a local search index. The only mail that leaves MailGent is what you've allowed, going to an agent you paired on this Mac.

What happens to mail once my agent receives it?

Then the agent's own privacy practices apply — MailGent can't control what an agent does with what you shared. That's why the defaults are tight and the Access Log shows every hand-off. Choose agents you trust.

Does it work with Gmail, iCloud or Outlook accounts?

If the account is in Apple Mail and its mail is downloaded, MailGent can read it. Signing in to Gmail and Yahoo directly is planned.

Which AI agents work with it?

Cursor and Grok Bot have built-in pairing today. Both connect over MCP, the standard protocol AI tools use to talk to apps.

Can the agent send or delete email?

No. In this alpha, agents can search, read what you allowed, and write drafts inside MailGent. MailGent never writes to Apple Mail. Sending and deleting will come later, and only with your approval each time.

Is it free?

Yes. The source is Apache-2.0 licensed on GitHub. The MailGent name and icon are trademarks, so forks need their own name.

Is it safe to use?

It's an early alpha, provided as is, without warranty. It only reads Apple Mail and never changes it, but treat it as a preview and keep your usual backups.
